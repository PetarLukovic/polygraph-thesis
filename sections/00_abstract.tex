\chapter*{Abstract}
\noindent
Robots, vehicles, and wearable devices that repeatedly traverse the same environment ---
a mapping robot's daily rounds, a fleet of cars driving through the same city, or a person
wearing an AR headset through the same building over many days --- each produce their own
trajectory estimate from onboard visual or visual-inertial odometry. These per-session
trajectories are locally consistent but drift over time and live in unrelated,
session-specific coordinate frames. Turning many such independent, drifting trajectories
into one globally consistent set of trajectories, wherever different sessions revisit the
same place, is the multi-session trajectory fusion problem this thesis addresses.

\noindent
Existing solutions to this problem largely operate offline: every session's data must be
collected and available up front, one session is often designated in advance as a fixed
reference frame, and only then is a single fused pose graph solved for. This assumption
breaks down for a continuously operating deployment, where sessions arrive one at a time and
there is no way to know in advance which one will end up anchoring the rest.

\noindent
This thesis presents Polygraph, a multi-session trajectory fusion pipeline that processes
sessions online, one keyframe and one closed spatial cluster at a time, without assuming
that all sessions are available up front or that any one session is a pre-designated
reference. Each session's odometry keyframes are grouped into local spatial clusters; when a
cluster closes, its keyframes are matched via learned image retrieval against every other
session's keyframes, and candidate cross-session pairs are verified through sparse feature
matching, monocular metric depth lifting, and robust pose estimation. A two-tier consensus
gate admits a cross-session loop-closure edge into the shared pose graph only once
independent evidence corroborates it, guarding against a single spurious match corrupting
the whole graph, while the graph itself is kept up to date through a cheap incremental solve
after every cluster and periodically refined through a robust, outlier-pruning batch solve.

\noindent
We evaluate Polygraph across three capture setups chosen to be as different from one another
as possible --- stereo-camera outdoor driving (KITTI), HoloLens visual-inertial indoor
walking, and Boston Dynamics Spot visual-inertial indoor legged locomotion --- with the two
indoor deployments built from existing localization/mapping benchmarks, CroCoDL and LaMAR
respectively, neither previously used to evaluate multi-session pose-graph fusion. On the one
hardware-matched, head-to-head comparison against the closest prior work we could run
(KITTI-00, identical RTX 4090), Polygraph runs about 2.9$\times$ faster (134.3s vs.\ 387.16s,
a real-time factor of 28.5\%), a real, honestly-reported trade-off: on that same sequence, it
currently commits fewer loop-closure edges than the prior work (142 vs.\ 517) --- a genuine,
unresolved gap this thesis surfaces and investigates rather than hides. We further evaluate on
Oxford RobotCar with RTK-verified ground truth, using a paired easy/hard session split ---
sharing all but one session, with the hard split adding a night traversal --- to test both
single- and multi-session performance under real day/night appearance change; results are
being finalized at the time of writing. \textbf{[TODO: report RobotCar headline numbers once
finalized.]} Together, these results show that robust multi-session trajectory fusion
is achievable in a genuinely online setting, without dense 3D reconstruction, across a
substantially wider range of real-world conditions than previously demonstrated.
