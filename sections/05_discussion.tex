\chapter{Discussion}
\label{chap:discussion}

\section{What the Results Show}
\label{sec:discussion-results}

Cross-session fusion helps most where a session genuinely revisits shared space and carries real
drift to correct. HGE session \texttt{hl\_568} goes from 2.09 meters raw to 0.29 meters once fused
with the rest of its component. HYDRO's total ATE roughly halves, from 0.73 to 0.34 meters, spread
across several sessions rather than one outlier. Where no real revisit exists, fusion cannot help
by construction. Every KITTI sequence except 00 and 07 stays identical between single-session and
multi-session mode, since those are the only two that ever meet again. Reference-anchored error
stays small everywhere it can be measured, 0.32 meters and 0.68 degrees on HGE, 0.17 meters and
0.34 degrees on HYDRO. Sessions end up not just individually accurate but placed correctly
relative to each other.

\section{Comparison to 2GO}
\label{sec:discussion-2go}

Polygraph and 2GO \cite{lim2025twogo} target the same underlying problem, large-scale pose-graph
optimization, extended here to genuinely independent sessions instead of one continuous capture,
and run online instead of offline (\mysecref{sec:relatedwork-2go}). Clustering and pose estimation
are not the contribution on their own. They exist to decide which candidate edges are even worth
proposing to the optimizer, admitting only the robust ones instead of every possible pair. On
KITTI's VINS-Fusion backbone the pipeline matches or beats 2GO's own published result on five of
seven sequences, and on VBR's single-session pose-estimation recipe it now beats 2GO's own
published aggregate across all 8 routes. The larger difference is speed. On 2GO's own slowest
reported case, \texttt{ciampino\_train0}, this pipeline finishes three and a half times faster
while committing 34 edges against their 3403. Filtering which edges reach the optimizer is a
direct cause of that speedup, not an unrelated side benefit.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion-limitations}

EuRoC is the one dataset where this pipeline shows a genuine limitation. Its rooms are small and
its own odometry is already close to ground truth before the pipeline ever runs. Single-session
and multi-session output end up close to those same raw odometry numbers, because there is little
drift left to correct. This is exactly the regime where a dense SLAM system like ORB-SLAM3's Atlas
has the most to work with, a fully maintained 3D map pays off most when the scene is small and
every session overlaps heavily with every other one. Atlas wins every split in
\mysecref{sec:experiments-main-euroc}, and the gap, while never larger than 12 centimeters, is a
genuine sign that this pipeline's sparse, map-less approach has less to offer once odometry is
already this good.

RobotCar's hard split shows a related but different pattern, VINS-Fusion multi-session mode
actually regresses there. This is not treated as a core limitation here. RobotCar's own odometry
is unusually poor to begin with, closer to a stress test than a typical deployment, and better
session splits are planned before drawing a firm conclusion from it.

\section{Design Choices}
\label{sec:discussion-design}

Three design choices stand out from the ablations. More cameras only help when they add genuinely
new viewpoints. HYDRO's rig spans nearly 360 degrees and cuts single-session ATE by nearly a
fifth, while KITTI's second camera, the other half of the same stereo rig, moves the result by
less than a centimeter. The choice of matcher and depth source barely matters for accuracy on
KITTI, HGE, and HYDRO once the full pipeline runs end to end, the original three methods land
within a few percent of each other on every one of those datasets. LightGlue stays the production
default because every alternative costs real wall time, up to 4.25 times as long in the worst
case, for no consistent accuracy gain, not because it is more accurate, and on HGE (iOS)
specifically, swapping either the matcher or the depth source can break cross-session anchoring
outright rather than just cost time. Retrieval method matters least of all. MegaLoc stays at or
near the best achievable ATE on three of the four locations tested, and the one place an
alternative edges it out (HGE HoloLens) is by a margin well inside normal run-to-run noise. HGE
(iOS) is the interesting exception, alternative retrieval methods do propose genuine new
cross-session candidate pairs there, yet none of it becomes a multi-session accuracy gain, only
MegaLoc's own edges clear the pipeline's downstream trust gates. More candidate edges are not
automatically useful ones.

\section{Implications and Future Work}
\label{sec:discussion-future}

A few results here are not final. The EuRoC anchoring table is missing a Maplab comparison,
planned for a later pass. RobotCar may still be re-evaluated on better session splits before its
results are treated as settled. None of this changes the two central findings above, cross-session
fusion helps wherever sessions genuinely revisit shared space, and doing so with far fewer, more
selective edges than 2GO or a dense map like Atlas needs is both accurate enough and substantially
faster.
